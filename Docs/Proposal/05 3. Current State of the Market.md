# 3. Current State of the Market

Existing vendors occupy most of the broad category; the opportunity lies in a narrower intersection.

## 3.1 Market taxonomy

| Category | Representative systems | Established capability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geometry and CAM engines | Sparrow, ProNest, SigmaNEST, Lantek, RADAN, JETCAM | True-shape nesting, machine/process constraints, toolpaths, cut sequencing, NC generation. |
| Remnant and stock management | Lantek, SigmaNEST, ProNest, JETCAM, RADAN | Track stock and offcuts, select remnants, maintain traceability, create new remnants. |
| Material planning and orchestration | AccuFit, Plataine, BySoft, CrossTrack | Match orders to inventory, mix jobs, schedule work, integrate ERP/MES and shop-floor state. |
| Vertical specialist systems | SlabWise and domain-specific optimizers | Batch jobs, map defects, preserve usable remnants, optimize vertical-specific workflow. |
| Academic multi-period optimization | Birgin, Cherri, Nascimento, Chen and related work | Usable leftovers, future periods, stochastic demand, sequential value correction, beam search. |

## 3.5 What is already established

- Tight irregular or true-shape 2D nesting.

- Exact or approximate remnant inventory, identification, and future reuse.

- Selection of a current remnant before opening a new sheet.

- Mixing compatible currently available work orders.

- Looking ahead at scheduled or released future work.

- Delaying work within due-date constraints to improve material yield.

- Deliberately shaping remnants and considering storage or handling costs.

- Machine scheduling, defect-zone avoidance, traceability, and ERP/MES integration.

A product limited to those claims would compete as another feature-rich CAM/MES system against mature vendors with installed bases, postprocessors, machine relationships, and shop-floor workflows. That is not an attractive initial entry strategy.

## 3.6 The narrower white space

| Potentially differentiated element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Exact irregular residual state | Value the actual connected polygonal remnants produced by each candidate, including holes and later quality zones. |
| Near-tied candidate comparison | Search for economic dispersion among layouts that are essentially equivalent on current material use. |
| Uncommitted demand | Use historical recurrence, quotes, customer signals, and uncertainty rather than only released work orders. |
| Conditional option value | Measure avoided future purchases given the complete inventory state, preventing double-counting of substitute remnants. |
| Chronological proof | Replay decisions with strict as-of-time information and compare with a perfect-information policy under the same action space. |
| CAM-agnostic overlay | Integrate above existing CAM rather than recreating toolpaths, machine logic, and NC postprocessors. |

## 3.7 Academic prior art

The research thesis is adjacent to a substantial usable-leftovers literature. Birgin, Romao, and Ronconi formulate multi-period two-dimensional non-guillotine cutting with usable leftovers and later introduce a forward-looking matheuristic that estimates the future utilization of leftovers. [12][15] Cherri and coauthors formulate usable leftovers under unknown future demand as a two-stage stochastic program with recourse. [13] Nascimento and coauthors study two-dimensional usable leftovers under demand uncertainty and report meaningful value of stochastic information in their tested setting. [14] Chen and Chen combine sequential value correction and beam search over multiple periods. [16]

These papers make two things clear. First, the basic principle is legitimate: future use can change which current cutting pattern is best. Second, YieldForge cannot claim to have invented stochastic future-aware cutting. The research contribution must be framed around exact irregular industrial geometry, modern high-performance candidate generation, conditional inventory-wide value, strong commercial baselines, and chronological validation.

## 3.8 Legal, licensing, and naming caveats

Sparrow is distributed under the MIT license. jagua-rs is distributed under MPL-2.0. [1][2] The proprietary simulation, valuation, and decision layers should remain modular, and a targeted licensing review should precede distribution of modified geometry components.

US8862261B1 addresses cutting-stock optimization across schedules and batches with lookahead and operational factors. [19] This proposal makes no infringement or freedom-to-operate conclusion; claim-level review is required before implementing the later scheduling layer. The perfect-information MVP is narrower, but the patent remains in the risk register.

The working name requires clearance. YieldForge.ai and YieldForge Agent are active DeFi-related uses; trademark, domain, search-visibility, and class conflicts remain unassessed. [20][21]
