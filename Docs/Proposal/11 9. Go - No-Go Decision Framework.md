# 9. Go / No-Go Decision Framework

The threshold is based on incremental net savings over a strong baseline, not on utilization or savings against discarding all scraps.

![[Attachments/image6.png]]

*Figure 6. Pre-registered evidence profile for deciding whether to continue beyond the perfect-information MVP.*

## 9.1 Quantitative thresholds

| Evidence profile | Signal | Required action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Target oracle <1.5% or unknown-future component <0.5 pp | Red / no-go | Publish or archive the MVP and stop commercial development. |
| Target oracle 1.5% to <2.5% or unknown-future component 0.5 to <1.5 pp | Yellow / validate | Obtain real manufacturer history; do not build the stochastic product yet. |
| Target oracle >=2.5% and unknown-future component >=1.5 pp | Green / continue | Proceed to stochastic valuation only if the supporting pass conditions also hold. |

## 9.2 Preferred pass condition

| Preferred result<br>At least 2.5% mean net oracle savings in a plausible target-customer regime, ideally 3% or more; at least 1.5 percentage points attributable to unknown future demand; immediate utilization sacrifice no greater than 0.5%; ordinary Sparrow candidate availability of at least 60%; and no dependence on optimistic terminal inventory credit. |
| --- |

A target-regime oracle gap of at least 2.5% is preferred because an eventual stochastic model will capture only part of the information advantage. The strongest result would be 3% or more, with at least 1.5 percentage points - ideally 2.0 points - attributable specifically to demand that was not yet a firm order. An oracle gap below 1.5% leaves too little tolerance for prediction error, physical recoverability, operator behavior, and integration friction.

## 9.3 Additional pass conditions

| Condition | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Strong comparator | Result is measured against the best frozen baseline with current-order pooling, remnant selection, regularity, age/FIFO, storage cost, and known-order lookahead. |
| Unknown-future differentiation | At least 1.5 percentage points of target-regime savings remain after the known-order lookahead baseline. |
| Economic asymmetry | Immediate utilization sacrifice is no more than 0.5%, and long-run savings materially exceed that sacrifice. |
| Candidate availability | At least 60% of valuable actions are present in ordinary near-optimal Sparrow candidate archives. |
| Opportunity frequency | The policy changes a meaningful share of eligible nests, ideally 20%-40%, rather than depending on rare outliers. |
| Remnant realization | At least 60% of deliberately preserved remnants are consumed within the modeled horizon under the oracle. |
| Distributed savings | The top 10 decisions contribute less than roughly 20%-25% of total savings. |
| Conservative inventory | Savings survive scrap-only or zero option value for terminal remnants and nominal storage/handling costs. |
| Operational remnants | A material portion survives minimum width, area, accessibility, and handling rules. |
| Broad enough evidence | Positive results appear across more than one geometry corpus and not only an exact-repeat toy regime. |
| Search credibility | Beam search is acceptably close to exhaustive small cases, or the reported opportunity is discounted by the approximation gap. |
| No-signal sanity | Random/no-recurrence controls remain within approximately 0.0%-0.3% systematic savings. |

## 9.4 Automatic stop conditions

- Target-segment perfect-information savings remain below 1.5% in plausible high-recurrence regimes.

- The incremental component attributable to unknown future demand remains below 0.5 percentage points after known-order lookahead.

- Savings disappear when the strongest commercial-style baseline is used.

- Savings require more than 1% immediate utilization sacrifice or depend on remnants that fail conservative recoverability rules.

- The no-signal control exceeds 0.5%, indicating a simulator, terminal-value, or accounting defect.

- Ordinary Sparrow candidate archives contain less than 40% of valuable actions and expanded search is impractical.

- Beam search cannot be validated and the result is too sensitive to beam width or arbitrary pruning.

- A small number of outlier streams or decisions accounts for most aggregate savings.

## 9.5 Interpretation examples

| Observed result | Decision meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Oracle 0.9%; unknown-future component 0.2 pp | Red. The differentiated economic ceiling is too small; publish/archive and stop. |
| Oracle 2.0%; unknown-future component 0.9 pp | Yellow. Obtain real shop history, but do not build the stochastic product yet. |
| Oracle 2.8%; unknown-future component 1.7 pp; sacrifice 0.4%; candidate availability 68% | Green. Proceed to stochastic valuation and seek a real-data partner. |
| Oracle 4.1% in a narrow high-value vertical; 1.2% elsewhere | Pursue the qualified vertical rather than a universal product. |
| Oracle 3.1%, but no-signal control 1.0% | Invalid. Diagnose leakage or accounting bias before interpreting the result. |

## 9.6 Final report format

The final artifact is a decision memo, not a demonstration video. It must state the strongest result, the most conservative result, the dependence on recurrence and cost assumptions, the baseline used, the search-quality gap, the operational recoverability gap, and the reason to continue or stop. The threshold will not be lowered after seeing the data.
