# 8. Benchmark and Experimental Program

The evidence must separate geometry opportunity, future-information value, search limitations, and operational assumptions.

## 8.1 Benchmark sources

| Source | Content | Use in YieldForge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sparrow benchmark instances | Irregular shapes and known solver behavior included or referenced by the Sparrow project. [1] | Candidate generation regression and geometry stress tests. |
| ESICUP irregular datasets | Classic instances including Albano, Dagli, Fu, Jakobs, Marques, Shapes, Shirts, Swim, Trousers, and related corpora. [17] | Diverse static polygon families and solver comparability. |
| Forward-looking usable-leftover code/instances | bromro2 and companion work for multi-period two-dimensional non-guillotine cutting. [12][15] | Simulator semantics and forward-looking policy comparison, though the formulations differ from irregular nesting. |
| Stochastic usable-leftover instances | Published multi-stage 2D cutting-stock instances with scenario trees and uncertain demand. [18] | Cost and scenario-accounting validation; rectangular structure is not a substitute for irregular benchmarks. |
| Synthetic sequential irregular suite | New timestamped streams built from real polygons with controlled recurrence, family variation, bundles, and regime shifts. | Primary evidence for the oracle opportunity under known ground-truth conditions. |

## 8.2 Why a new temporal benchmark is necessary

Public nesting datasets are primarily static: they provide polygons and stock dimensions but not chronological order arrivals, quotes, inventory history, remnant creation, storage, or later reuse. Published usable-leftover datasets provide valuable multi-period structure but often use rectangles or guillotine constraints. The central experiment therefore requires a synthetic temporal layer built from real irregular geometries.

Synthetic data is acceptable for the first falsification test only if the generator is transparent, controls include no-signal regimes, parameters are varied widely, and the final claim is limited to opportunity under modeled conditions. A positive synthetic result does not authorize product development; it authorizes acquisition of real manufacturer history.

## 8.3 Temporal demand regimes

| Regime | Construction | Expected diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No-signal control | Future shapes are independent or nearly unique. | Oracle advantage should approach zero after storage and terminal accounting. |
| Exact recurrence | A small set of identical part families returns repeatedly. | Upper-bound favorable regime and geometry plumbing check. |
| Variant recurrence | Related shapes vary in scale, contour, quantity, or allowed rotation. | More realistic family-level reuse test. |
| Correlated bundles | Orders arrive as recurring assemblies or kits of related parts. | Tests complementary demand rather than independent part arrivals. |
| High-mix custom | Many unique shapes with weak family structure. | Likely low-value customer archetype; tests stop conditions. |
| Highly repetitive | Strong recurrence, high volume, expensive stock, and frequent compatible remnants. | Likely target-customer archetype. |
| Regime shift | Previously common families decline and new families emerge. | Tests inventory overproduction and horizon sensitivity. |

## 8.4 Core parameter grid

The full factorial space is too large to run naively. Candidate generation will be cached, and the program will use a two-stage design: a screening matrix to identify sensitive variables, followed by a concentrated paired evaluation around plausible customer regimes.

| Variable | Levels | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate sacrifice epsilon | 0%, 0.1%, 0.25%, 0.5%, 1%, 2% | Measures the price paid today to create future options. |
| Candidate budget | Multiple seeds / progressive incumbents; low, medium, high compute | Determines whether search diversity limits the opportunity. |
| Remnant threshold | Minimum area and minimum-width combinations | Separates idealized leftovers from operationally plausible inventory. |
| Family recurrence | 0% through strong recurrence | Primary explanatory variable for future value. |
| Family variation | Exact, affine variants, contour variants, bundle variants | Tests geometric generalization. |
| Order volume and horizon | Short, medium, long streams | Measures accumulation, reuse, and terminal effects. |
| Storage and handling | Zero, nominal, conservative | Tests whether savings survive inventory friction. |
| Scrap and stock prices | Low, nominal, high material value | Identifies economic segments. |
| Rotation rules | Restricted versus flexible | Changes substitutability and fit opportunity. |

## 8.5 Primary metric

| Primary outcome<br>Net purchased-material cost relative to the strongest as-of-time baseline, including storage and handling, subtracting realized scrap proceeds, and applying a conservative terminal-inventory rule. |
| --- |

Average current nest utilization is a diagnostic, not the decision metric. A future-aware policy is permitted to show slightly worse utilization on the current sheet if it reduces cumulative purchased material across the horizon.

## 8.6 Supporting metrics

| Metric family | Reported measures |
| --- | --- |
| Purchased stock | Sheets opened, area purchased, and purchase cost. |
| Waste | Scrap area, scrap value, and process loss. |
| Remnant behavior | Count created, count reused, reuse rate, average lifetime, ending inventory. |
| Tradeoff | Immediate utilization sacrificed, long-run savings, and savings-to-sacrifice ratio. |
| Search | Candidate diversity, oracle A vs B gap, beam vs exact gap, compute time. |
| Distribution | Mean, median, lower-tail outcome, fraction of streams with positive savings, and outlier contribution. |
| Segmentation | Savings by geometry corpus, recurrence, material price, volume, and remnant policy. |

## 8.7 Experimental controls

- Paired streams: every policy sees the same order events, initial inventory, geometry, costs, and candidate archives.

- Common seeds: any random candidate generation is persisted before policy evaluation.

- No-signal control: the oracle must not generate systematic apparent value where future geometric information is absent.

- Closed-horizon accounting: ending remnants receive scrap value or no option credit in the primary analysis; alternate terminal values are sensitivity tests.

- Calibration/test separation: baseline tuning and thresholds are frozen before final benchmark evaluation.

- Exact small cases: beam-search quality is measured rather than assumed.

- Ablations: remove remnant regularity, storage cost, current-order pooling, and expanded search one at a time to explain the source of gains.

- No cherry-picking: all pre-registered regimes and seeds are reported, including negative results.

## 8.8 Statistical interpretation

Results will be paired at the stream level. The report will provide means, medians, bootstrap confidence intervals, and the distribution of savings across streams. A positive average driven by a few extreme cases is not sufficient. The lower confidence bound and the proportion of positive streams matter because a commercial customer needs repeatable savings, not a lottery ticket.

The benchmark is a controlled simulation rather than an estimate of the universal market. Results will be stated conditionally: for example, "under recurring-family regimes with these costs and remnant constraints, the perfect-information policy reduced purchased material by X%." Generalization to real shops requires subsequent historical data.

## 8.9 Diagnostics the experiment must produce

- Information gap: strong baseline cost minus perfect-information cost using the same candidate set.

- Search gap: perfect-information cost using ordinary candidates minus perfect-information cost using expanded search.

- Recoverability gap: geometric upper-bound savings minus conservative operational-remnant savings.

- Long-horizon gap: rollout oracle minus beam-search oracle.

- Baseline gap: simple myopic policy minus strongest commercial-style baseline.

- Terminal-value sensitivity: result under zero, scrap-only, and bounded continuation value for ending remnants.

## 8.10 Expected signal profile by benchmark regime

A credible result should not show the same uplift everywhere. The oracle advantage should increase as geometric recurrence, remnant substitutability, material value, and operational flexibility increase, while the random/no-recurrence control remains near zero. The following ranges are pre-registered evidence targets, not predicted results.

| Benchmark regime | Compelling oracle range | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Random / no recurrence | 0.0%-0.3% | Correct negative control; the system should have almost no exploitable future information. |
| High-mix custom / weak recurrence | 0.3%-0.8% | Limited opportunity is expected; unlikely to support a broad product. |
| Moderate recurrence | 1.0%-1.5% | The mechanism is emerging but still requires a high-spend customer segment. |
| Strong recurrence | 1.5%-2.5% | Commercially interesting; warrants real historical data. |
| High recurrence + known-order pooling | 2.5%-3.5% | Strong result within the MVP scope; enough headroom for a later stochastic policy. |
| High-value material + high recurrence | 3.0%-5.0%+ | Compelling vertical opportunity. |
| Best identifiable customer phenotype | >=4.0% | Major opportunity if the phenotype can be recognized before integration. |

## 8.11 Supporting metric gates

The percentage saving is necessary but insufficient. The benchmark must also demonstrate that the effect is inexpensive to obtain, distributed across many decisions, operationally realizable, and available in ordinary Sparrow candidate populations.

| Metric | Green target | Yellow / investigate | Red concern |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Target-segment oracle advantage | >=2.5%; ideal >=3.0% | 1.5%-<2.5% | <1.5% |
| Unknown-future component | >=1.5 pp; ideal >=2.0 pp | 0.5-<1.5 pp | <0.5 pp |
| Immediate utilization sacrifice | <=0.25% ideal; <=0.5% acceptable | >0.5%-1.0% | >1.0% |
| Opportunity frequency | 20%-40% of eligible nests changed | 10%-20% | <10% or isolated events |
| Preserved-remnant realization | >=70% ideal; >=60% minimum | 40%-<60% | <40% |
| Savings concentration | Top 10 decisions <20% of savings | 20%-35% | >50% |
| Ordinary Sparrow candidate availability | >=70% ideal; >=60% minimum | 40%-<60% | <40% |
| No-signal control | 0.0%-0.3% | >0.3%-0.5% | >0.5% |
