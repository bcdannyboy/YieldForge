# 4. YieldForge End-State Opportunity

The end-state is a material-intelligence layer coordinating geometry, inventory, demand, and execution.

## 4.1 Product definition

YieldForge would sit above the factory's ERP/MRP, inventory records, CAM systems, quote pipeline, and machine state. It would request or generate multiple feasible actions, calculate the exact material state created by each action, estimate the downstream value of that state, and recommend the action minimizing expected cumulative cost.

The product should initially complement, not replace, systems such as SigmaNEST, ProNest, Lantek, RADAN, or JETCAM. The incumbent CAM remains responsible for machine-specific process validation, lead-ins and lead-outs, common-line rules, thermal constraints, cut sequencing, and NC generation. YieldForge supplies the economic objective and chooses among process-feasible alternatives.

## 4.2 End-to-end workflow

1. Observe released orders, due dates, quotes, historical demand, full stock, exact remnants, material metadata, prices, lead times, machine constraints, and current shop state.

1. Form candidate batches and stock assignments within due-date and process constraints.

1. Ask Sparrow, a proprietary geometry engine, or the native CAM to generate multiple process-feasible nests rather than one immediate optimum.

1. Extract the exact physically recoverable remnant polygons and carry forward their material, quality, direction, age, cost, and provenance.

1. Evaluate each resulting inventory state across scenarios of future demand, purchases, remnant allocation, storage, handling, and scrap.

1. Select the risk-adjusted action with the lowest expected cumulative cost and produce a human-readable decision explanation.

1. Send the selected nest to the incumbent CAM for final validation and NC generation.

1. Record physical execution, actual remnant creation, later reuse, and realized savings to update the model and prove value.

## 4.3 Product modules

| Module | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Material state service | Canonical full-sheet/remnant inventory with exact geometry, metadata, location, provenance, and quality fields. |
| Candidate gateway | Adapters for Sparrow and commercial CAM systems; normalizes candidate nests and compute budgets. |
| Remnant geometry service | Computes recoverable polygons, remnant topology, fit signatures, and subsequent remnant-as-stock nesting. |
| Future value engine | Scenario rollout or learned approximation of marginal future cost reduction conditional on complete inventory. |
| Rolling material planner | Selects batch, stock, nest, machine, and timing under due-date, setup, and capacity constraints. |
| Lifecycle manager | Revalues, reserves, locates, and disposes remnants according to storage cost, scrap value, and changing demand. |
| Evidence layer | Chronological counterfactuals, A/B shadow mode, realized savings attribution, and executive reporting. |

## 4.4 Ideal initial customer profile

- At least USD 10 million, and preferably USD 25 million or more, in annual eligible sheet, plate, slab, fabric, foam, honeycomb, or composite material spend.

- Recurring or related part families rather than purely one-off geometry.

- Meaningful volumes of remnants, offcuts, or short rolls that are retained but imperfectly reused.

- Expensive or lead-time-sensitive material where a one-percentage-point improvement is financially material.

- A mature CAM/MES environment capable of exporting stock, orders, candidate nests, and execution results.

- Enough operational flexibility to mix compatible orders or reserve remnants without compromising due dates.

- An executive sponsor willing to measure savings against a counterfactual baseline rather than accept utilization screenshots.

## 4.5 Economic significance of small percentages

| Annual eligible material spend | 0.5% saving | 1.0% saving | 2.0% saving |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| USD 5M | USD 25k | USD 50k | USD 100k |
| USD 10M | USD 50k | USD 100k | USD 200k |
| USD 25M | USD 125k | USD 250k | USD 500k |
| USD 50M | USD 250k | USD 500k | USD 1.0M |
| USD 100M | USD 500k | USD 1.0M | USD 2.0M |

At 1%, the value proposition becomes straightforward for large shops: a manufacturer spending USD 25 million annually on eligible material has a gross opportunity of roughly USD 250,000 per year before software fees and implementation cost. At 0.25%, the product requires much larger spend and becomes difficult to distinguish from normal operating variance. This is why the validation threshold is set aggressively.

## 4.6 Commercial packaging

| Mode | Description | Commercial implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shadow audit | Ingest historical or live read-only data; quantify missed remnant opportunities and customer-specific oracle gap. | Lowest integration risk; strongest initial sales proof. |
| Advisory overlay | Score candidate nests from incumbent CAM and return a recommendation with expected value and confidence. | Avoids replacing CAM; depends on candidate diversity and APIs. |
| Orchestration layer | Vary order pools, stock choices, objectives, and CAM calls; select the globally preferred plan. | More value and control; greater integration burden. |
| Geometry mode | Generate complete nests through a Sparrow-derived engine and pass them into CAM for validation and execution. | Maximum algorithmic control; highest certification and process risk. |
| Full shop planner | Coordinate batching, machine assignment, remnant reservation, purchasing, and rolling schedules. | Largest opportunity; not justified until the narrow value signal is proven. |

## 4.7 Potential defensibility

- A longitudinal corpus linking exact part geometry, order arrival, candidate nests, remnant creation, later reuse, purchase avoidance, and realized operational cost.

- A conditional remnant value function that understands substitutes and complements across the complete material inventory.

- A candidate-diversification method that searches residual topology rather than merely rerunning compactness optimization.

- CAM-normalization adapters and a process-feasibility feedback loop across heterogeneous industrial systems.

- Customer-specific chronological simulators that qualify opportunity before deployment and measure realized savings afterward.

- Operational knowledge about which theoretically usable remnants are actually recovered, found, moved, and consumed.

The moat would not be Sparrow itself. Sparrow is open source. The defensible asset would be the economic state model, empirical data, integration layer, search guidance, and demonstrated ability to capture future option value in real operations.
