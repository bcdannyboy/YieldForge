# 5. Validation Thesis and Falsifiable Hypotheses

The first program is successful only if it produces a decision, including a credible decision to stop.

## 5.1 Primary validation question

| Core question<br>If every future order were known perfectly, how much net purchased-material cost could be avoided by selecting future-aware nests and remnants rather than applying the strongest policy available with only information known at the time? |
| --- |

## 5.2 Why perfect information is the correct first test

Forecasting error can only reduce achievable value. Perfect information therefore measures the size of the opportunity before the project invests in predicting it. If the oracle gap is 0.3%, a practical model capturing half the available signal produces only 0.15% before integration and operational losses. If the oracle gap is 2.5%, a practical system can lose substantial value to forecast error and still remain commercially relevant.

The benchmark will not be called a mathematical upper bound unless exact optimization is achieved. The scalable oracle uses rollout and beam search over a constrained action space. It is a perfect-information policy benchmark: it knows the realized future but may still miss the globally optimal sequence. Small tractable instances will be solved exhaustively to measure that approximation gap.

## 5.3 Pre-registered hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Falsifiable statement |
| --- | --- |
| H1 - Candidate dispersion | Near-optimal Sparrow layouts with <=1% immediate material-cost difference will create materially different future inventory value. |
| H2 - Recurrence dependence | Oracle savings will increase monotonically with geometric family recurrence and will approach zero in the no-signal control. |
| H3 - Asymmetric trade | In target regimes, cumulative material savings will exceed the immediate utilization sacrifice by at least three to one. |
| H4 - Strong-baseline survival | The oracle advantage will remain after remnant-first stock selection, regularity heuristics, current-order co-nesting, storage cost, and conservative terminal treatment. |
| H5 - Search sufficiency | Ordinary multi-seed Sparrow candidates will expose at least part of the opportunity; expanded candidate diversity will reveal whether search, rather than information, is the binding constraint. |
| H6 - Operational robustness | A nontrivial portion of the advantage will survive conservative remnant eligibility rules rather than depending on idealized internal voids or unusable slivers. |

## 5.4 Scope frozen for the experiment

- One cutter and no machine scheduling.

- One uniform material, grade, thickness, and surface-quality class per benchmark stream.

- One standard rectangular new-stock size, with unlimited availability at a fixed purchase cost.

- Every decision epoch contains a current released batch that must be fulfilled immediately. Multi-sheet jobs are deterministically decomposed into sheet-sized sub-batches before policy comparison.

- All currently released compatible parts may be co-nested; unreleased future orders may not be advanced, delayed, or batched by the baseline.

- Fixed kerf, clearance, allowed rotations, and minimum remnant rules.

- Existing exact remnants can be selected and recursively cut into new remnants.

- No purchasing lead times, quotes, stochastic predictions, heterogeneous quality fields, operator schedules, or CAM postprocessing.

## 5.5 Information sets

| Policy | Information available | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Myopic geometry policy | Current released batch, current inventory, current candidate nests. | Chooses best immediate material utilization. |
| Commercial-style baseline | Current released compatible work, current inventory, remnant age/regularity, storage and scrap parameters. | Uses strong current-state heuristics and the best-performing baseline variant. |
| Known-order lookahead baseline | All firm orders that are already released or scheduled as of the decision timestamp. | Uses deterministic known work but no later realized orders. |
| Perfect-information rollout | The entire realized future stream, but only for evaluating the current candidate; later decisions follow a strong deterministic policy. | Computationally tractable first oracle. |
| Perfect-information beam search | The entire realized future stream at every decision, within a pruned beam of inventory states. | Stronger sequential oracle; validated against exact small cases. |
