# 2. Problem Statement and Optimization Thesis

The current nest is a decision about future inventory, not merely a packing result.

## 2.1 The immediate-utilization blind spot

A typical nesting objective rewards the current layout for fitting more part area into less stock. This is rational but incomplete. The unused material left by a nest becomes one of three things: immediate scrap, a retained remnant that is later consumed, or a retained remnant that occupies space and is eventually scrapped. Current utilization does not distinguish between those futures.

Consider two feasible layouts for the same batch. Layout A achieves 94.0% immediate utilization but leaves several disconnected slivers. Layout B achieves 93.2% utilization and leaves one connected shape whose dimensions and contours closely match a recurring future part family. The current-period metric prefers A. The economically correct choice depends on whether B avoids enough future purchasing and handling cost to justify the 0.8 percentage-point sacrifice today.

| Measure | Layout A | Layout B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate utilization | 94.0% | 93.2% |
| Residual geometry | Disconnected channels and pockets | One contiguous recoverable irregular remnant |
| Current-period material use | Best | Slightly worse |
| Probability of future geometric fit | Low | High in recurring-family shop |
| Expected long-run cost | Potentially higher | Potentially lower despite current sacrifice |

## 2.2 The true state variable is the entire inventory

A remnant does not possess a fixed standalone price. Its value depends on every other piece of compatible stock already available. If the shop already holds three remnants capable of satisfying the same likely future order, producing a fourth may add almost no option value. If the shop has no substitute and procurement lead times are long, the same geometry can be highly valuable.

| Conditional value principle<br>MarginalValue(R \| I) = FutureCost(I) - FutureCost(I union R)<br>where I is the complete compatible material inventory, not an empty inventory. |
| --- |

This non-additivity is central. Independently pricing each remnant and summing the results systematically overvalues redundant inventory. YieldForge must evaluate the inventory state produced by a candidate nest, not attach an isolated score to each leftover polygon.

## 2.3 The objective

The production decision should minimize cumulative material economics. In the end-state system the decision may include order batching, stock selection, machine assignment, nest selection, and timing. In the MVP, the action is deliberately narrowed to selection among current candidate nests and compatible stock objects.

| Validation objective<br>Score(candidate) = current purchase and process cost<br>+ future purchased-material cost<br>+ storage and handling cost<br>- scrap proceeds<br>+ conservative terminal-inventory adjustment<br>OracleSavings = (BaselineCost - OracleCost) / BaselineCost |
| --- |

## 2.4 Geometry, not area, determines substitutability

A ten-square-foot remnant can be less useful than a seven-square-foot remnant. Area, bounding box, convexity, minimum width, connectivity, permitted rotation, kerf, grain direction, and exact polygonal fit determine whether future parts can actually use it. The validation system therefore treats remnants as exact irregular containers and uses geometric feasibility rather than area alone.

The system also distinguishes theoretical unused material from a recoverable remnant. Empty space inside a nest may be trapped in a skeleton, fragmented into narrow channels, too small to handle, or inaccessible without an additional trim cut. The first system will include both a geometric upper-bound extractor and a conservative operational extractor so that apparent savings are not created by impossible inventory.

## 2.5 Material properties are a spatial field

The eventual material model must retain more than material name, grade, and thickness. Some stock can contain weak zones, surface defects, heat-affected regions, certification boundaries, variable thickness, grain direction, or local inspection uncertainty. A remnant created from that stock inherits the clipped property field. A future high-strength part must not be placed over a weak region merely because the outer geometry fits.

This capability is intentionally deferred from the MVP. The initial schema will reserve fields for material and quality regions so the architecture does not need to be redesigned later, but the experiment will use one uniform material, grade, and thickness. jagua-rs already supports irregular containers, holes, inferior-quality zones, and hazard filters, making it a plausible basis for later spatial compatibility rules. ## 2.6 The broader shop-level problem

Across a factory with multiple cutters, the decision expands from one nest to a rolling material-allocation and production-dispatch problem. The planner can decide which released orders to combine, which remnants to reserve, which cutter receives a batch, and whether a short delay improves co-nesting without missing due dates. Decisions interact because two machines can otherwise create redundant remnants for the same probable future demand.

This broader opportunity is commercially important but methodologically dangerous at the start. Adding scheduling could create savings unrelated to future remnant option value and make a negative result uninterpretable. The first program therefore prohibits deliberate waiting, future-order batching, and multi-machine assignment. It isolates the nest-selection effect.
